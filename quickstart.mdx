Preparing for senior React interviews requires more than memorizing hook signatures. Top engineering teams assess:
1. **Reconciliation & Diffing Mental Models**: Can you explain how Fiber processes component trees and detects changes?
2. **Re-render Optimization**: Can you stop unnecessary re-renders using composition without defaulting blindly to `useMemo` and `useCallback`?
3. **Async Race Conditions & Edge Cases**: Can you prevent stale UI states, unhandled errors, and out-of-order responses?
4. **React 19 Primitives**: Are you up to speed on Actions, optimistic mutations, and modern server/client paradigms?

## Recommended 3-Step Study Plan

<Steps>
  <Step title="1. Internalize the 30-Second Elevator Pitch">
    Every topic starts with a concise senior summary. Practice stating this out loud. Interviewers ask broad questions like *"How do re-renders work?"* or *"When would you use useLayoutEffect?"*—delivering a razor-sharp opening sentence establishes seniority immediately.
  </Step>

  <Step title="2. Review Pitfalls & The Anti-Pattern Spotlights">
    Interviewers frequently present buggy code snippets and ask: *"What is wrong with this component?"*
    Review the **Pitfalls & Anti-Patterns** section on each page to instantly spot bugs like:
    - Component definitions inside render functions
    - Mutating state variables expecting synchronous updates
    - Derived state placed in `useEffect` instead of computed during render
    - Missing cleanups on timers and AbortControllers
  </Step>

  <Step title="3. Drill the Senior Interview Q&A Accordions">
    Each page contains real-world interview questions categorized by difficulty (`Common`, `Tricky`, `Senior`). Test yourself before expanding each accordion to evaluate your depth of understanding.
  </Step>
</Steps>
